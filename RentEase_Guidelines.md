## 1. Property Management

### Understanding Properties & Units

In RentEase, a **Property** is the main building or location (like an apartment building, condominium, or boarding house), while **Units** are the individual rentable spaces within that property (like rooms, apartments, or floors).

**Example:** "Sunshine Apartments" (Property) → Unit 1A, Unit 1B, Unit 2A (Units)

### Property Information (Shared by All Units)

**Basic Details:**
- **Title** - Property name for listings
- **Type** - Apartment, Condominium, Boarding House, or Single House
- **Main Image** - Featured photo of the property

**Address & Location:**
- **Street, Barangay, Zip Code**
- **City or Municipality**
- **Map Coordinates** - For Google Maps location

**Additional Info:**
- **Nearby Institutions** - Schools, hospitals, malls, transport, banks, churches, etc.
- **Other Information** - Custom notes about the property

### Unit Information (Unique to Each Unit)

**Basic Details:**
- **Label** - Unit identifier (e.g., "Unit 3A", "Room 5")
- **Description** - Detailed unit description
- **Floor Number** - Which floor the unit is on
- **Max Occupancy** - How many people can stay

**Pricing & Settings:**
- **Target Price** - Monthly rental rate
- **Requires Screening** - Enable tenant screening
- **Unit Condition** - Good, Under Maintenance, or Unusable

**Media:**
- **Main Image** - Primary unit photo
- **Other Images** - Up to 6 additional photos

**Features:**
- **Amenities** - WiFi, AC, parking, etc.
- **Lease Rules** - Visitor, pet, noise, parking policies, etc.

### Shared Property Information

**IMPORTANT:** The property's **main image** and **address** are shared by all units within that property. When a tenant views any unit listing, they will see the property's main image and address.

**Main Property Image:**
- This is typically the building exterior or a representative photo of the property
- It appears on all unit listings belonging to this property
- **Tip:** Choose a clear, well-lit photo of your building's facade that helps tenants identify the property when they visit

**Property Address:**
- The full address (street, barangay, city/municipality, zip code) is set at the property level
- All units under this property automatically use this address
- **Tip:** Ensure your address is accurate and complete. This helps tenants find your property and appears in search results

### How It Works Together

**Key Point:** Each unit inherits the property's address, main image, and nearby institutions. But each unit has its own price, photos, amenities, condition, and lease rules.

### Best Practices

- ✓ **Use Descriptive Names** - Name your properties and units clearly (e.g., "Green Valley Residences - Studio Unit A") so tenants can easily identify them
- ✓ **Upload Unit-Specific Photos** - While the main property image is shared, each unit should have its own photos showing the interior, layout, and unique features
- ✓ **Keep Information Updated** - Regularly update property and unit information to reflect current conditions, prices, and availability
- ✓ **Complete All Details** - Fill in all fields for both property and units. Complete listings attract more tenants and get approved faster

## 2. Listing Best Practices

### What is a Listing?

A **Listing** is an advertisement for your unit that makes it visible to prospective tenants browsing on RentEase. When you create a listing, your unit's information (along with its parent property details) becomes searchable and viewable by tenants looking for their next home.

**Key Points About Listings:**
- A listing is created for a **specific unit** within a property
- The listing displays both property info (address, main image) and unit info (price, amenities)
- Listings require payment and admin review before becoming visible
- Each listing has a lifecycle status that tracks its current state
- You can optionally make your listing **Featured** for more visibility

### Listing Lifecycle Statuses

#### WAITING_REVIEW
Payment complete, waiting for admin review. Your listing is in the queue and will be reviewed by our team to ensure it meets our guidelines.

#### VISIBLE (Goal Status)
Publicly visible and active! Your listing has passed review and is now live on the platform, visible to all tenants browsing for properties.

#### HIDDEN
Temporarily hidden by you. You can hide your listing at any time (e.g., when the unit is temporarily unavailable). You can make it visible again when ready.

#### EXPIRED
Listing duration has ended. Your listing's advertising period has expired. You can renew or extend the listing to make it visible again.

#### FLAGGED (Warning)
Found suspicious and will be hidden. Our AI moderation or admin review has detected potential issues. The listing is temporarily hidden pending further review. You will be notified of the specific concerns.

#### BLOCKED (Serious)
Fully removed or deactivated due to violations. Your listing has been permanently blocked due to serious policy violations. Blocked listings cannot be restored and may result in account penalties.

### Featured Listings

**Featured listings** get priority placement in search results and browse pages, making them more visible to prospective tenants.

**Benefits of Featured:**
- Appears at top of search results
- Special "Featured" badge displayed
- Higher visibility to tenants
- Faster tenant inquiries

**How to Get Featured:**
- Select "Featured" option when creating listing
- Pay the featured listing fee
- Pass admin review
- Your listing gets priority placement

### AI Content Moderation

Our platform uses **AI-powered content moderation** to automatically detect and flag prohibited content before admin review. This helps maintain a safe and trustworthy platform.

**What AI Checks For:**
- **Scam patterns** - Requests for upfront online payments
- **Fake info** - Misleading or false information
- **Discriminatory** - Content that discriminates
- **Illegal content** - Prohibited activities
- **Inappropriate** - Explicit or offensive content
- **Other violations** - Policy breaches

**What Happens If Flagged?**
If AI detects issues, your listing will be flagged and the problematic content will be logged for admin review. You'll be notified and given a chance to correct the issue before resubmitting.

### How to Get Your Listing Approved

Follow these guidelines to ensure your listing quickly passes review and becomes **VISIBLE**:

**Quality Photos:**
- Upload clear, well-lit photos of the actual unit
- Include interior shots showing rooms, layout, and amenities
- Use recent photos that reflect current condition
- Avoid stock photos or images from other sources
- Take photos during daytime for best lighting

**Accurate Information:**
- Provide honest, detailed descriptions that match the property
- Set realistic rental prices with no hidden fees
- Ensure all fields are complete and accurate
- List actual amenities available in the unit
- Keep lease rules clear and reasonable

**Complete All Details:**
- Fill in property address completely
- Add max occupancy and floor number
- Select applicable amenities
- Set unit condition accurately
- Enable screening requirement if needed

### How to Avoid Getting Flagged or Blocked

To prevent your listing from being flagged or blocked, **strictly avoid** the following:

**Scamming Patterns:**
- **NEVER** request upfront online payments before property viewing. Our AI automatically detects and blocks such requests. Always meet tenants in person first.

**Discriminatory Information:**
- Do not include any language that discriminates based on race, religion, gender, age, disability, or other protected characteristics.

**False or Misleading Information:**
- Do not misrepresent the property - location, size, amenities, condition, or availability. All information must be accurate and truthful.

**Inappropriate Content:**
- Do not use inappropriate, explicit, or offensive images as property pictures. Use only property-related photos.

**Duplicate or Fake Listings:**
- Do not create multiple listings for the same unit or listings for properties that don't exist or aren't available for rent.

**Important Warning:**
Repeated flagging may lead to **BLOCKED** status. Blocked listings cannot be restored, and serious violations may result in **account suspension or termination**.

### Quick Tips for Success

- ✓ Review your listing before submission
- ✓ Use high-quality, well-lit photos
- ✓ Write clear, professional descriptions
- ✓ Set competitive, honest pricing
- ✓ Respond promptly to admin requests
- ✓ Keep your account in good standing
- ✓ Update listings regularly
- ✓ Consider Featured for faster results

## 3. Tenant Screening

### What is Tenant Screening?

**Tenant Screening** is a verification process that allows landlords to gather and review important information about prospective tenants **before signing a lease agreement**. This helps landlords make informed decisions and find reliable tenants.

### How the Process Works

1. **Landlord Invites Tenant** - Landlord sends a screening invitation to a prospective tenant's email
2. **Tenant Fills Out Form** - Tenant completes the screening form with personal, employment, and rental history info
3. **AI Analyzes Submission** - Our AI system generates a risk assessment and summary based on the provided information
4. **Landlord Reviews & Decides** - Landlord reviews the screening report and approves or rejects the application

**Enable Screening for Your Units:**
To use tenant screening, enable the **"Requires Screening"** option when creating or editing your unit. This allows you to invite prospective tenants for screening before signing a lease.

### Screening Status Flow

Each screening application goes through the following statuses:

#### PENDING
Invitation has been sent to the tenant. Waiting for the tenant to complete and submit the screening form.

#### SUBMITTED
Tenant has submitted their screening form. The landlord can now review the information and AI assessment to make a decision.

#### APPROVED
Landlord has approved the tenant's application. The tenant is cleared to proceed with the lease signing process.

#### REJECTED
Landlord has declined the tenant's application. A reason or remark may be provided to the tenant.

**Status Flow:** PENDING → SUBMITTED → APPROVED or REJECTED

### Information Collected from Tenants

The screening form collects the following information to help landlords assess tenant suitability:

**Basic Information:**
- Full Name
- Birthdate
- Employment Status (Student, Employed, Self-Employed, Unemployed)
- Income Source (Salary, Business, Allowance)
- Monthly Income

**Document & Identity Verification:**
*(Tenant confirms availability of these documents - landlord may verify in person)*
- Has Valid Government ID
- Has NBI Clearance
- Has Proof of Income

**Employment History:**
- Current Employer
- Job Position/Role
- Years Employed
- Employment Remarks

**Rental History:**
- Previous Landlord Name
- Previous Landlord Contact
- Previous Rental Address
- Reason for Leaving
- Eviction History (Yes/No)
- Late Payment History (Yes/No)

**Lifestyle Indicators:**
*(Used to assess compatibility with property rules)*
- Smokes (Yes/No)
- Drinks Alcohol (Yes/No)

### AI Risk Assessment

After a tenant submits their screening form, our **AI system analyzes the information** and generates a risk assessment to help landlords make informed decisions.

**AI Generates:**
- **Risk Score** - 0.0 to 1.0 (lower is better)
- **Risk Level** - LOW, MEDIUM, or HIGH
- **Summary** - Readable assessment result
- **Detailed Findings** - By category

**AI Evaluates:**
- Financial stability
- Employment history
- Identity verification
- Document availability
- Rental history
- Eviction risk
- Payment behavior
- Lifestyle compatibility
- Overall assessment

**Risk Level Indicators:**
- **LOW** - Good candidate
- **MEDIUM** - Some concerns
- **HIGH** - Review carefully

**AI is a Tool, Not a Decision Maker:**
The AI assessment is meant to **assist** landlords, not replace their judgment. Always review the full application and consider meeting the tenant in person before making your final decision.

### Best Practices for Landlords

- ✓ **Verify Documents in Person** - The screening form confirms document availability. Always verify actual documents (ID, NBI clearance, proof of income) when meeting the tenant in person.
- ✓ **Contact Previous Landlords** - If the tenant provided previous landlord contact info, reach out to verify rental history and get a reference.
- ✓ **Consider the Full Picture** - Don't rely solely on the AI risk score. Consider the tenant's overall situation, communication, and your personal interaction during property viewings.
- ✓ **Provide Clear Feedback** - If rejecting an application, provide a brief, respectful remark explaining the decision. This maintains professionalism and helps tenants understand.

## 4. Communication

### In-App Messaging System

RentEase provides a built-in **messaging system** that allows landlords and tenants to communicate directly within the platform. Each conversation between a landlord and tenant is organized into a **Chat Channel**.

### How It Works

1. **Tenant Initiates Contact** - When a tenant is interested in a listing, they can send an inquiry message to the landlord
2. **Chat Channel Created** - A unique conversation channel is created between the tenant and landlord
3. **Ongoing Communication** - Both parties can exchange messages, discuss details, and coordinate viewings

**Chat Channel Contains:**
- Tenant & Landlord participants
- Channel status (Inquiry, Active, Ended)
- Last message preview
- Read status tracking

**Each Message Contains:**
- Message content
- Sender information
- Timestamp (when sent)
- Read timestamp (when viewed)

### Channel Status Types

Each chat channel has a status that reflects the current state of the conversation:

#### INQUIRY (Default)
Initial status when a tenant first reaches out about a listing. The conversation is in the inquiry phase - typically discussing property details, availability, and scheduling viewings.

#### ACTIVE
The conversation is ongoing and both parties are actively communicating. This typically happens after initial interest is confirmed and discussions are progressing toward a potential lease.

#### ENDED
The conversation has concluded. This could mean the tenant found another place, the landlord filled the unit, or both parties decided not to proceed.

**Typical Channel Flow:** INQUIRY → ACTIVE → ENDED

### Benefits of In-App Messaging

- **Conversation History** - All messages are saved and organized. Easily refer back to previous discussions about terms, agreements, or property details.
- **Read Receipts** - Know when your messages have been read. This helps you understand if the other party has seen your message.
- **Organized Conversations** - Each tenant-landlord pair has a dedicated channel. No mixing up conversations or losing track of who said what.
- **Documentation** - Messages serve as a record of discussions. Useful if there are any disputes about what was discussed or agreed upon.
- **Privacy Protection** - Communicate without sharing personal phone numbers or email addresses until you're ready.
- **Notifications** - Get notified when you receive new messages so you can respond promptly.

### Response Time Expectations

Good communication is key to successful landlord-tenant relationships. Here are recommended response times:

**General Inquiries:**
- Respond within **24-48 hours**. Quick responses show professionalism and increase chances of securing good tenants.

**Viewing Requests:**
- Respond within **24 hours**. Tenants often have multiple options and may move on quickly.

**Urgent Matters:**
- Respond **as soon as possible**. Issues like maintenance emergencies or lease concerns need immediate attention.

**Ongoing Discussions:**
- Keep the conversation flowing. Don't leave messages unanswered for extended periods.

### Communication Best Practices

- ✓ **Be Professional and Courteous** - Maintain a respectful tone in all communications. First impressions matter, and professionalism builds trust.
- ✓ **Be Clear and Specific** - Provide clear information about property details, availability, pricing, and requirements. Avoid vague responses that lead to follow-up questions.
- ✓ **Respond Promptly** - Quick responses show you're engaged and reliable. Set aside time daily to check and respond to messages.
- ✓ **Keep Records of Important Agreements** - If you discuss terms, pricing, or special arrangements, summarize them in a message so both parties have a record.
- ✓ **Set Communication Boundaries** - Let the other party know your preferred communication hours. It's okay to set expectations about response times.

### Things to Avoid

- ✗ **Sharing Sensitive Information Too Early** - Don't share bank account details, personal IDs, or other sensitive information through chat until you've verified the other party and are ready to proceed.
- ✗ **Requesting Payments Through Chat** - Never request or send payments through the messaging system. Always handle payments in person with proper documentation.
- ✗ **Ignoring Messages** - Even if you're not interested, respond to let the other party know. Ghosting damages your reputation and wastes everyone's time.
- ✗ **Unprofessional Language** - Avoid rude, aggressive, or inappropriate language. This can result in account warnings or suspension.

## 5. Lease Management

### What is a Lease?

A **Lease** is a formal rental agreement between a landlord and tenant that establishes the terms and conditions for renting a unit. In RentEase, leases help you track rental periods, payments, and tenant relationships.

### Key Components of a Lease

- **Property & Unit** - Which property and unit is being rented
- **Tenant & Landlord** - Parties involved in the agreement
- **Rent Amount** - Monthly rental fee
- **Lease Period** - Start and end dates
- **Payment Schedule** - When rent is due
- **Security Deposit** - One-time deposit amount

### Lease Types

Choose the appropriate lease type based on the rental period and agreement terms:

#### STANDARD (Default)
Standard rental agreement for typical residential leases. Most common type for apartments, condominiums, and houses.

#### SHORT_TERM
For rentals with shorter durations, typically less than 6 months. Common for temporary stays, vacation rentals, or transitional housing.

#### LONG_TERM
For extended rental periods, typically 1 year or longer. Suitable for tenants seeking stability and landlords wanting consistent occupancy.

#### FIXED-TERM
Lease with a specific, non-renewable end date. Both parties agree to the exact duration with no automatic renewal.

### Lease Status Flow

Each lease progresses through different statuses during its lifecycle:

#### PENDING (Editable)
Lease has been created but not yet activated. Waiting for both parties to confirm or sign the agreement.

**Editable:** You can still modify lease details, terms, and information while in PENDING status.

#### ACTIVE (Locked)
Lease is active and ongoing. Tenant is currently renting the unit, and rent payments are being tracked.

**Not Editable:** Once a lease becomes ACTIVE, the lease details cannot be modified. Only payments and notes can be added.

#### COMPLETED (Fully Locked)
Lease has ended successfully after passing the end date. The rental period is complete, and all obligations have been fulfilled.

**Fully Locked:** All lease information, payments, and data are locked and cannot be edited. This is a permanent status.

**Note:** When a lease passes its end date, you can manually set it to COMPLETED or TERMINATED status.

#### TERMINATED (Fully Locked)
Lease was ended early before the scheduled end date, or after passing the end date. This could be due to mutual agreement, breach of contract, or other circumstances.

**Fully Locked:** All lease information, payments, and data are locked and cannot be edited. This is a permanent status.

**Note:** When a lease passes its end date, you can manually set it to TERMINATED if it didn't complete successfully, or COMPLETED if it ended normally.

#### CANCELLED
Lease was cancelled before it became active (while still in PENDING status). This typically happens if the agreement falls through before the start date.

**Note:** Cancelled leases never became active, so they remain in a cancelled state with limited functionality.

**Lease Status Flow & Editability:**
- PENDING (Editable) → ACTIVE (Locked)
- When lease passes end date: COMPLETED (Fully Locked) or TERMINATED (Fully Locked)

**Important:** Once a lease becomes ACTIVE, it cannot be edited. If a lease passes its end date, you can set it to COMPLETED or TERMINATED. Once in COMPLETED or TERMINATED status, all information (including payments) is permanently locked.

### Payment Intervals & Due Dates

**Payment Intervals:**
- **MONTHLY (Default & Only Option)** - Rent is due monthly. The system is designed for monthly rental payments only. Weekly and daily payment intervals are not available.

**Due Date (Day of Month):**
- Set which day of the month rent is due (1-28). The system automatically adjusts if the due date falls on a day that doesn't exist in shorter months.
- **Example:** If you set due date to 5, rent will be due on the 5th of each month. If you set it to 31, it will adjust to the last day of months with fewer days.

**Security Deposit:**
- A one-time amount held by the landlord until the lease ends. This is separate from monthly rent and is typically returned (minus any deductions) when the tenant moves out.
- **Important Reminder:** Security deposits are for your record-keeping only. RentEase does NOT process or hold deposits. Handle all deposit transactions directly with tenants and provide proper documentation.

### Lease Documents

You can upload a digital copy of your signed lease agreement document. This serves as a record and reference for both parties.

**Best Practices for Lease Documents:**
- Upload a signed copy of the lease agreement
- Ensure the document is clear and readable
- Keep physical copies as well for your records
- Update the document if lease terms change

### Landlord Notes (Private)

Landlord Notes are **private observations** that only you (the landlord) can see. Tenants cannot view these notes. Use them to track tenant behavior, issues, or important observations throughout the lease period.

**Note Categories:**
- CLEANLINESS
- NOISE
- BEHAVIOR
- COMMUNICATION
- PROPERTY_DAMAGE
- OTHER

**Privacy & Use:**
These notes are for your personal record-keeping and documentation. They help you track patterns, document issues, and maintain a history of the tenancy. Use them responsibly and objectively.

### Payment Tracking

Payment tracking allows landlords to record and monitor all payments related to a lease. This includes rent payments, penalties, adjustments, and other charges that occur during the contract period.

**Payment Status:**

**PENDING (Editable):**
- Payment has been created but not yet marked as paid. This is the default status for new payment records.
- **Can be edited or deleted:** PENDING payments can be modified or removed before they are marked as paid.

**PAID (Locked):**
- Payment has been marked as paid. The payment record is now permanent and cannot be modified.
- **Cannot be edited or deleted:** Once a payment is marked as PAID, it is permanently locked and cannot be changed or removed.

**Payment Types:**
- **RENT** - Regular monthly rent payment
- **ADVANCE PAYMENT** - Payment made before the due date
- **PREPAYMENT** - Payment for future rent periods
- **PENALTY** - Late payment fees or other penalties
- **ADJUSTMENT** - Corrections or adjustments to amounts

**Timing Status:**
- **ONTIME** - Paid on or before due date
- **LATE** - Paid after due date
- **ADVANCE** - Paid before due date

**Creating Payment Records:**
Landlords can create new payment records for various purposes during the lease contract:
- **Regular Rent:** Create monthly rent payment records
- **Penalties:** Record late payment fees or other penalties incurred during the contract
- **Adjustments:** Add corrections or adjustments to payment amounts
- **Other Charges:** Record any additional payments that occur during the lease period

**Important Reminders:**
- **Payment tracking is for record-keeping only.** RentEase does NOT process payments between landlords and tenants.
- **Handle all payments directly** with tenants outside the platform.
- **Mark payments as PAID** only after you have actually received the payment.
- **PENDING payments can be edited or deleted** before marking them as paid.
- **PAID payments are permanent** and cannot be modified or deleted.
- **Use payment notes** to add context or details about specific payments.

### Lease Management Best Practices

- ✓ **Start Renewal Discussions Early** - Begin discussing lease renewal at least 30 days before the end date. This gives both parties time to decide and prevents last-minute vacancies.
- ✓ **Keep Accurate Records** - Document all lease terms, payments, and important communications. This helps resolve disputes and provides a clear history.
- ✓ **Monitor Lease Status** - Regularly check your leases to see which are expiring soon, which are active, and which need attention.
- ✓ **Use Lease Nicknames** - Give your leases descriptive nicknames (e.g., "John - Unit 3A") to easily identify them in your lease list.
- ✓ **Track Payments Separately** - Use the payment tracking feature to record rent payments, but remember that RentEase does not process payments - handle all transactions directly with tenants.

## 6. Financial Tracking

### What is Financial Tracking?

**Financial Tracking** is a tool designed to help landlords understand their current financial position across their rental properties. It allows you to record, monitor, and analyze income and expenses to get a clear picture of your property's profitability.

**Important: Record-Keeping Tool Only**
Financial tracking is **NOT a payment processor**. It's a tool for your personal record-keeping to help you understand where you are financially. All payments are handled directly between you and tenants outside the platform.

**Track Income:**
- Record all money received from rentals, fees, deposits, and other income sources.

**Track Expenses:**
- Record all money spent on maintenance, repairs, utilities, taxes, and other property-related costs.

### Transaction Types

#### Income Categories

- **RENT** - Regular rental income from tenants
- **LATE_FEE** - Late payment fees charged to tenants
- **DEPOSIT** - Security deposits received
- **OTHER_INCOME** - Any other income sources

#### Expense Categories

- **MAINTENANCE** - Regular property maintenance costs
- **REPAIRS** - Property repair expenses
- **UTILITIES** - Water, electricity, internet, etc.
- **INSURANCE** - Property insurance premiums
- **TAXES** - Property taxes and related fees
- **PROPERTY_MANAGEMENT** - Property management service fees
- **LISTING_ADVERTISING** - Listing fees and advertising costs
- **OTHER_EXPENSE** - Any other property-related expenses

### Property-Level vs Unit-Level Transactions

**Property-Level Transactions:**
Expenses or income that apply to the entire property, not a specific unit.
- Property-wide maintenance
- Building insurance
- Property taxes
- Common area utilities

**Unit-Level Transactions:**
Expenses or income specific to a particular unit within a property.
- Rent from specific unit
- Unit-specific repairs
- Unit utilities
- Unit maintenance

### Recurring Transactions

For transactions that happen regularly, you can set them as **recurring**. The system will automatically calculate the total amount based on how many times the transaction occurs within your selected date range.

**Recurring Intervals:**
- **DAILY** - Every day
- **WEEKLY** - Every week
- **MONTHLY** - Every month
- **YEARLY** - Every year

**How Recurring Transactions Work:**
When you set a transaction as recurring, the system calculates how many times it occurs within your selected date filter (This Month, This Year, or All Time). For example, a monthly rent of ₱10,000 set as MONTHLY will show as ₱10,000 × 12 = ₱120,000 when viewing "This Year".

### Financial Metrics & Insights

The Financial Overview provides key metrics to help you understand your property's financial health:

**Total Income:**
- Sum of all income transactions (rent, fees, deposits) within your selected period.

**Total Expense:**
- Sum of all expense transactions (maintenance, repairs, utilities) within your selected period.

**Net Profit:**
- Income minus expenses. Shows whether your property is profitable (positive) or losing money (negative).

**Profit Margin:**
- Percentage of income that remains as profit. Calculated as (Net Profit / Income) × 100%.

**Visual Timeline Chart:**
The timeline chart shows income and expenses over time, helping you visualize trends and patterns. You can view data by day (This Month), by month (This Year), or by year (All Time).

### Filtering & Date Ranges

**Date Filters:**
- **This Month** - Current month's transactions
- **This Year** - Current year's transactions
- **All Time** - All historical transactions

**Scope Filters:**
- **All Properties** - View transactions across all properties
- **Specific Property** - View transactions for one property (property-level and all units)
- **Specific Unit** - View transactions for one specific unit

**Type Filters:**
- **Income Only** - Show only income transactions
- **Expense Only** - Show only expense transactions

### Managing Transactions

**Creating Transactions:**
To record a new transaction, click "Record Transaction" and fill in:
- **Type:** Income or Expense
- **Property:** Which property (required)
- **Unit:** Optional - leave as "Property-level" or select a specific unit
- **Amount:** Transaction amount in PHP
- **Date:** When the transaction occurred
- **Category:** Select appropriate category
- **Description:** Brief description (max 15 words)
- **Recurring:** Optional - set if transaction repeats

**Description Word Limit:**
Transaction descriptions are limited to **15 words maximum**. Keep descriptions concise and clear (e.g., "Monthly rent payment from Unit 3A").

**Editing & Deleting Transactions:**
- Click on any transaction in the table to edit it. You can modify all fields including type, amount, date, category, and description.
- You can delete any transaction record using the delete button. This action cannot be undone, so make sure you want to remove the record permanently.

### PDF Financial Reports

Generate professional PDF reports of your financial data for record-keeping, tax preparation, or sharing with accountants.

**Report Options:**
- **All Properties:** Generate a report for all your properties combined
- **Specific Property:** Generate a report for one property (includes all units)
- **Date Range:** This Month, This Year, or All Time

**What's Included in the PDF:**
- Summary statistics (Total Income, Total Expense, Net Profit, Profit Margin)
- Detailed transaction table with all information
- Recurring transaction calculations
- Property and unit information
- Date range and generation timestamp
- Professional RentEase branding

### Best Practices

- ✓ **Record Transactions Regularly** - Enter transactions as they occur to maintain accurate, up-to-date financial records. Don't let them pile up.
- ✓ **Use Appropriate Categories** - Categorize transactions correctly to get meaningful insights. This helps you understand where your money is coming from and going to.
- ✓ **Set Recurring for Regular Items** - Use recurring transactions for monthly rent, regular maintenance, or other predictable income/expenses. This saves time and ensures accuracy.
- ✓ **Review Financial Metrics Regularly** - Check your net profit and profit margin regularly to understand your property's financial health and make informed decisions.
- ✓ **Generate Reports for Tax Season** - Use the PDF report feature to generate comprehensive financial reports for tax preparation or accounting purposes.

**Remember: This is a Tool, Not a Payment Processor**
Financial tracking helps you understand your financial position, but all actual payments are handled directly between you and tenants. Keep receipts and documentation for all transactions.

## 7. Maintenance Requests

### What are Maintenance Requests?

**Maintenance Requests** allow tenants to report issues with their rental units directly to landlords through the platform. This system streamlines communication, ensures issues are tracked, and helps landlords prioritize and manage repairs efficiently.

**For Tenants:**
- Submit maintenance requests with photos and descriptions
- Track the status of your requests and receive notifications when landlords update them

**For Landlords:**
- Receive notifications for new requests
- Update status as you work on issues
- Maintain a complete history of all maintenance activities

### Maintenance Request Status Flow

Maintenance requests progress through different statuses as landlords work on resolving them:

#### OPEN
The initial status when a tenant submits a maintenance request. The landlord has been notified and can review the issue.

**Actions:** Landlord can update to IN_PROGRESS, RESOLVED, or INVALID. Tenant can cancel (only if OPEN).

#### IN_PROGRESS
The landlord has started working on the maintenance issue. The unit's condition is automatically set to UNDER_MAINTENANCE.

**Actions:** Landlord can update to RESOLVED or INVALID. Cannot revert to OPEN.

#### RESOLVED
The maintenance issue has been fixed. The unit's condition is automatically set back to GOOD (if no other active maintenance requests exist).

**Final Status:** Cannot be changed once set to RESOLVED.

#### CANCELLED
The tenant has cancelled the maintenance request. Only tenants can cancel requests, and only if the status is OPEN.

**Final Status:** Cannot be changed once set to CANCELLED.

#### INVALID
The landlord has marked the request as invalid (e.g., duplicate request, not a real issue, or outside scope of maintenance).

**Final Status:** Cannot be changed once set to INVALID.

**Important Status Rules:**
- Final statuses (RESOLVED, INVALID, CANCELLED) cannot be changed once set
- Only tenants can cancel requests, and only if status is OPEN
- Landlords cannot set status to CANCELLED (only tenants can)
- When status changes to IN_PROGRESS, unit condition becomes UNDER_MAINTENANCE
- When status changes to RESOLVED, unit condition becomes GOOD (if no other active requests)

### For Tenants: Creating Maintenance Requests

As a tenant, you can submit maintenance requests for issues in your rental unit.

**Requirements:**
- **Active Lease:** You must have an active lease for the unit you're requesting maintenance for
- **Property & Unit:** Select the property and specific unit where the issue is located
- **Description:** Provide a clear, detailed description of the maintenance issue
- **Photo:** Upload a photo showing the issue (highly recommended for clarity)

**What Happens When You Submit:**
- The landlord receives a notification about your maintenance request
- The request status is set to **OPEN** automatically
- You can track the status of your request and receive updates when the landlord changes it

**Cancelling Requests:**
You can cancel a maintenance request, but only if its status is **OPEN**. Once a landlord starts working on it (IN_PROGRESS) or marks it as RESOLVED/INVALID, you cannot cancel it.

### For Landlords: Managing Maintenance Requests

As a landlord, you receive notifications when tenants submit maintenance requests.

**Available Actions:**

**Update to IN_PROGRESS:**
- When you start working on the issue, update the status to IN_PROGRESS. This automatically sets the unit condition to UNDER_MAINTENANCE.

**Mark as RESOLVED:**
- Once the issue is fixed, mark it as RESOLVED. The unit condition automatically returns to GOOD (if no other active requests exist).

**Mark as INVALID:**
- If the request is invalid (duplicate, not a real issue, or outside scope), mark it as INVALID. This is a final status.

**Cannot Cancel:**
- Landlords cannot cancel requests. Only tenants can cancel, and only if the status is OPEN.

**Status Update Rules:**
- You can update OPEN requests to IN_PROGRESS, RESOLVED, or INVALID
- You can update IN_PROGRESS requests to RESOLVED or INVALID
- You cannot change final statuses (RESOLVED, INVALID, CANCELLED)
- You cannot revert IN_PROGRESS back to OPEN
- Tenants are automatically notified when you update the status

**Unit Condition Updates:**
The system automatically updates unit conditions based on maintenance request status:
- **IN_PROGRESS:** Unit condition becomes UNDER_MAINTENANCE
- **RESOLVED:** Unit condition becomes GOOD (only if no other active maintenance requests exist for that unit)

### Maintenance Request Information

Each maintenance request contains the following information:

### Best Practices

**For Tenants:**
- ✓ **Provide Clear Descriptions** - Describe the issue in detail, including location, severity, and when it started. This helps landlords understand and prioritize the request.
- ✓ **Include Photos** - Photos help landlords see the issue clearly and assess the urgency. Take clear, well-lit photos from multiple angles if possible.
- ✓ **Report Issues Promptly** - Don't wait for small issues to become big problems. Report maintenance issues as soon as you notice them.
- ✓ **Be Patient** - Landlords may need time to assess and schedule repairs. Check the status of your request, but allow reasonable time for resolution.

**For Landlords:**

- ✓ **Prioritize Urgent Issues** - Address safety issues, water leaks, and heating/cooling problems immediately. Less urgent issues can be scheduled appropriately.
- ✓ **Update Status Regularly** - Keep tenants informed by updating the status as you progress. This builds trust and reduces follow-up inquiries.
- ✓ **Mark as Resolved When Complete** - Once the issue is fixed, mark it as RESOLVED. This automatically updates the unit condition and notifies the tenant.
- ✓ **Use INVALID Appropriately** - Only mark requests as INVALID if they are truly invalid (duplicates, not real issues, or outside maintenance scope). Communicate with tenants if needed.

## 8. Safety Tips & Fraud Prevention

### Important Safety Warning

⚠️ **As a landlord, it's crucial to remain vigilant against fraudulent tenants and scams.** Always verify tenant information, use secure payment methods, conduct proper screening, and report suspicious behavior immediately.

**If you encounter suspicious activity:**
- Do not proceed with any lease or transaction
- Report the tenant or application immediately
- Contact RentEase support
- If you've been scammed, contact local authorities

### Common Fraudulent Activities to Watch For

**Fake Tenant Applications:**
Scammers may submit applications with fake identities, forged documents, or stolen personal information to gain access to properties.

**Overpayment Scams:**
A "tenant" sends a check or payment for more than the required amount and asks you to refund the difference. The original payment is fake and will bounce, leaving you out of money.

**Identity Theft Attempts:**
Scammers may try to obtain your personal information, property details, or financial information through fake inquiries or applications.

**Property Damage or Theft:**
Fraudulent tenants may cause intentional damage, steal appliances or fixtures, or use the property for illegal activities.

**Fake References or Documents:**
Scammers may provide fake employment letters, bank statements, or references from accomplices posing as previous landlords or employers.

**Squatting or Illegal Occupancy:**
Fraudulent tenants may move in without proper lease documentation or refuse to leave after lease expiration, making eviction difficult.

### How to Protect Yourself

- ✓ **Use Tenant Screening** - Always use the platform's tenant screening feature. Verify identity, employment, income, and rental history. Don't skip background checks, even if a tenant seems trustworthy.
- ✓ **Verify All Documents** - Check employment letters, bank statements, and references independently. Call employers and previous landlords directly using publicly listed numbers, not numbers provided by the applicant.
- ✓ **Meet Tenants in Person** - Always meet prospective tenants in person before signing a lease. Verify their identity matches their application. Be cautious of tenants who refuse to meet or only communicate online.
- ✓ **Require Proper Lease Documentation** - Never allow tenants to move in without a signed lease. Ensure all terms are clearly documented, including rent amount, deposit, lease duration, and responsibilities. Keep copies of all documents.
- ✓ **Be Wary of Overpayments** - If a tenant sends more than required and asks for a refund, this is almost always a scam. Wait for payments to fully clear before refunding any amount. Better yet, only accept exact amounts.
- ✓ **Check Payment Methods** - Use secure, traceable payment methods. Be cautious of checks, money orders, or wire transfers from unknown sources. Wait for payments to clear before providing keys or access.
- ✓ **Protect Your Personal Information** - Don't share sensitive information like bank account details, social security numbers, or passwords. Use the platform's secure messaging system for communication.
- ✓ **Conduct Regular Property Inspections** - Schedule regular inspections (with proper notice) to ensure the property is being maintained and not used for illegal activities. Document the property condition regularly.
- ✓ **Trust Your Instincts** - If something feels off about an applicant or situation, trust your instincts. Don't let pressure or urgency rush you into a decision. There are always other tenants.

### Red Flags to Watch For

- Tenant refuses to provide identification or screening information
- Overpayment with request for refund
- Rush to move in without proper documentation
- Unwillingness to meet in person
- Fake or suspicious documents
- References that can't be verified
- Unusual payment methods or requests
- Poor communication or evasive answers
- Inconsistent information in application

### General Safety Tips for Landlords

**Protect Your Account:**
- Use a strong, unique password
- Enable two-factor authentication if available
- Never share your login credentials
- Log out from shared devices

**Verify Tenant Information:**
- Verify tenant identity through multiple sources
- Check employment and income independently
- Verify contact information and references
- Research tenant background before approving

**Document Everything:**
- Keep copies of all tenant communications
- Save payment receipts and transaction records
- Document property condition before and after
- Keep signed lease and contract copies

**Report Suspicious Activity:**
- Report fraudulent tenants immediately
- Flag suspicious applications or behavior
- Contact RentEase support
- File police reports if scammed

### What to Do If You've Been Scammed

**Immediate Actions:**

1. **Stop all communication** with the fraudulent tenant immediately
2. **Document everything:** Save all messages, emails, screenshots, application documents, and transaction records
3. **Report to RentEase:** Contact support immediately with all evidence
4. **Report to authorities:** File a report with local police and relevant fraud agencies
5. **Contact your bank:** If you've received fraudulent payments or sent refunds, contact your bank immediately
6. **Secure your property:** If a fraudulent tenant has access, take appropriate legal steps to secure your property
7. **Monitor your accounts:** Watch for unauthorized transactions or identity theft

**Information to Provide:**
- Tenant username or profile information
- Screenshots of conversations and applications
- Transaction records and payment details
- Property address and unit information
- Tenant screening reports and documents
- Timeline of events
- Any other relevant evidence

**Remember:** While RentEase works to prevent fraud, you are ultimately responsible for verifying tenant information and conducting proper screening. Always exercise caution, verify all documents independently, and use the platform's screening tools before approving any tenant or signing a lease.
